Case Study

Wayfair – Product Discovery & PDP Modernization

Role: Frontend / UI Engineer  ·  Product: Large-scale e-commerce product discovery & detail page experience

Overview

Wayfair is a high-traffic e-commerce platform where the product listing page (PLP) and product detail page (PDP) have a direct impact on discovery, conversion, and revenue. I worked on modernizing parts of the PLP/PDP experience, focusing on React-based component architecture, performance, and close collaboration with UX and product teams.

The goal was to make product discovery clearer on mobile and desktop, improve accessibility, and support continuous experimentation through A/B tests.

Tech Stack

React TypeScript Next.js / SSR GraphQL Apollo Client Design System Components A/B Testing & Feature Flags

My Responsibilities

  • Implemented and refactored PLP/PDP React components using the internal design system, ensuring consistency and accessibility.
  • Worked with UX designers to translate Figma specs into responsive layouts and interaction patterns that work well on both mobile and desktop.
  • Integrated GraphQL data (via Apollo) for product details, inventory, and personalization signals, while keeping the UI fast and resilient.
  • Helped set up and maintain experiment variants for A/B testing, collaborating with product and analytics teams.

Design Process

1. Understanding the current experience

We started by reviewing the existing PLP and PDP flows and looking at analytics and customer feedback. A few recurring issues surfaced:

  • On mobile, key product information (price, delivery estimate, reviews) was easy to miss.
  • The PDP layout had grown over time, making it harder to scan and compare options.
  • Some core interactions (such as variant selection or adding to cart) were not fully accessible via keyboard and screen readers.

2. Collaborating on layouts & component structure

I worked closely with UX and product to break down the PLP/PDP into modular sections: gallery, core product info, price & promotions, delivery, and recommendations. We used the design system where possible, and identified areas where new components were needed.

Before building, we aligned on:

  • The information hierarchy on different breakpoints.
  • Which elements needed to remain visible as users scrolled (sticky “Add to Cart” on mobile, for example).
  • Edge cases such as out-of-stock items, missing images, or limited-time promos.

3. Iterating through A/B testing

Once new components and layouts were implemented, they were rolled out behind feature flags and tested as A/B experiments. I helped:

  • Implement different layout variants for hero sections and information blocks.
  • Add tracking hooks so that product and analytics teams could measure behavior and conversion changes.
  • Refine the UI based on experiment results and qualitative feedback from UX research sessions.

Engineering Details

  • Used Next.js server-side rendering (SSR) / incremental static regeneration to keep product pages indexable and fast, improving LCP and SEO.
  • Built PDP sections as composable React components, wired with GraphQL queries via Apollo, so individual pieces (e.g. delivery info, recommendations) could evolve independently.
  • Ensured components followed accessibility best practices (ARIA attributes, keyboard navigation, focus management) to meet internal standards.
  • Participated in code reviews and worked with QA to validate behavior across browsers and devices.

Impact & Learnings

The refactored PLP/PDP experience made it easier for customers to scan and evaluate products, particularly on mobile, and gave the business a more flexible foundation for ongoing experimentation.

The project reinforced how important it is to:

  • Keep the visual hierarchy aligned with user decision-making.
  • Treat the design system as a shared language between designers and engineers.
  • Use experiments not only to optimize metrics, but also to validate UX assumptions and guide future iterations.
